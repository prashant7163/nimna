Security model

Written for technical readers. No marketing tone past this line. Protocol spec: v0.1 draft in the repo.

Summary

Nimna is a synchronous, end-to-end encrypted, account-less 1:1 messaging protocol. A stateless WebSocket relay bridges two live device connections and forwards opaque frames. There is no store-and-forward: if both peers are not connected, nothing is transmitted and nothing is queued server-side. All persistent state lives on the two devices, encrypted at rest.

Pairing

  1. Device A generates a fresh channel keypair and a short single-use pairing code C (unambiguous alphabet, default TTL 24 h, configurable shorter). A registers on the relay under the rendezvous topic H(C) — the relay sees only the hash, never the code.
  2. The code travels out-of-band (in person, a call, paper — your choice, your threat model).
  3. Device B generates its own fresh keypair and registers on H(C). The relay bridges the two connections; the devices perform an authenticated key agreement through it.
  4. Both devices derive a short safety phrase from the agreed key material and display it. You read it to each other over a channel you trust. A mismatch means a man-in-the-middle joined with your code — you discard and re-pair. Verification is strongly encouraged, not enforced.
  5. Both devices derive an opaque channel routing ID (CRID) from the shared key material, persist the channel locally, and discard the code permanently. Codes are single-use; the rendezvous topic dies when both sides disconnect.

Known pairing risk, stated plainly: a third party who intercepts the code before your peer uses it can pair with the initiator in their place. Mitigations: code TTL, single use, and the safety-phrase check. If your code-sharing channel is hostile, verify the phrase before saying anything that matters.

Sessions

Either side may knock on a paired channel. The relay forwards the knock if the peer holds a live connection, or returns PEER_OFFLINE; knocks expire after roughly 60 seconds. On accept, the relay bridges the two connections in memory and forwards frames verbatim. Frames are [CRID][opaque ciphertext] — typing indicators, acknowledgments, and hangups are encrypted control frames indistinguishable from messages at the relay. Either side disconnecting or hanging up tears down the bridge; the relay forgets the CRID immediately.

Messages composed while the peer is offline are stored only on the sender's device, marked waiting, and transmitted in order at the next bridge. To the network this is indistinguishable from live typing.

Cryptography

The v0.1 spec fixes requirements; the concrete, audited primitives are being finalized for v0.2 and will be published in the spec with test vectors before launch. We commit to boring, widely reviewed constructions — no novel cryptography, ever. The requirements the chosen scheme must satisfy:

  • authenticated key agreement bridgeable through an untrusted relay,
  • AEAD confidentiality and integrity on every frame,
  • forward secrecy across sessions at minimum; per-message ratcheting is the target since sessions are live,
  • a human-comparable safety phrase derived from the agreed key,
  • replay protection within sessions,
  • availability in well-audited libraries on both device and relay platforms.

Until v0.2 is published, treat cipher-level claims here as unfinished by our own admission. The architecture-level claims (no storage, no accounts, synchronous-only) are structural and inspectable today.

On-device storage

  • Channel keys in the platform keystore (Secure Enclave / Android Keystore); history in a local database encrypted at rest.
  • Excluded from iCloud/Google backups by default. No cloud sync exists.
  • Panic wipe destroys a channel's keys and history irrecoverably. With forward-secret primitives, previously captured ciphertext stays undecryptable after key destruction.
  • Disappearing messages are enforced by both clients, honor-system by nature; the UI says so explicitly rather than implying a guarantee.

Metadata — the honest section

The relay necessarily observes: connecting IP addresses, connection timing, ciphertext sizes/timing, and opaque CRIDs. It holds these in memory for live connections only, writes nothing to disk, and keeps no production logs. This is a meaningful residual surface:

  • A network observer learns that your device talked to the nimna relay, and when.
  • A global or relay-position observer could attempt traffic correlation between two endpoints (who talks to whom, when). v1 does not defend against this; we document it instead of hand-waving. Federation and transport overlays are future work.
  • Push, if opted in, involves Apple/Google push infrastructure delivering a fixed contentless string; the push token is the only piece of state that may outlive a connection, and push is default-off.
