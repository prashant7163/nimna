# Nimna — Logo Concepts (2.1)

All three concepts share one construction rule: **the wordmark is not typeset — it is drawn**, monoline, every stroke the same weight with rounded ends, because the brand's only motif is a line. Lowercase `nimna` per decision record. All concepts pass the Hard Avoids list (no locks, shields, spy anything).

Preview: open [`logo-previews.html`](logo-previews.html) — dark, light, and 48px app-icon tile tests.

---

## Concept 1 — "Two Ends" · `concept-1-two-ends.svg`

**Symbol:** two dots with a short lit line between them: `●──●`. Two people, the line, nothing else — the positioning sentence drawn in three strokes.
**Wordmark detail:** the i-dot is amber — *you*, the near end of the line. The symbol and the wordmark tell the same story at two scales.
**At 48px:** dots-and-line stays legible; on a near-black tile it reads as a quiet glyph, not an app trying to be noticed.
**Risk:** the most "designed" of the three — two brand elements (symbol + hot i-dot) to govern.

## Concept 2 — "The Dash" · `concept-2-the-dash.svg`

**Symbol:** a single short amber dash. The entire brand reduced to one lit stroke — a line that is *on*. Wordmark fully neutral; the dash is the only color anywhere.
**At 48px:** unbeatable — a dash cannot break. The most discreet option: on a home screen it's nearly punctuation.
**Risk:** abstract to the point of mute; the "two people" half of the story is gone. Also the closest to generic (a dash could belong to anyone).

## Concept 3 — "The Headstroke" · `concept-3-headstroke.svg`

**Symbol/structure:** one continuous line above the whole word — the Devanagari *shirorekha* abstracted, honoring the Sanskrit root of the name. The letters hang beneath it, as निम्न would.
**At 48px:** icon form is a line with two stems hanging from it (two people on the same line) — legible and ownable.
**Risk:** the line above the word is subtle enough that the Devanagari reference may read as just an underline misplaced; the story needs telling. (A construction note: an earlier draft extended the stems to touch the line, true to Devanagari — it destroyed legibility ("hlmhd") and was rejected.)

---

## Recommendation: Concept 1 — "Two Ends"

It is the only concept that carries the full sentence — *two people, one line* — in both the symbol and the wordmark, it animates naturally (the line between the dots is the session-state line from the color system), and it survives the 48px discretion test. Concept 2 is the runner-up and effectively lives *inside* Concept 1 (the dash is Concept 1's line without its ends); if Concept 1 ever feels busy at tiny sizes, the symbol degrades gracefully to the dash.

**Refinement plan if selected (2.2):** clear-space = one dot-diameter on all sides; minimum sizes; mono (single-color) variant with neutral line; inverse for light mode (amber-ink line); wordmark-only and symbol-only lockups.
