F6 — Receiving a knock (in-app · via content-free push · knock screen)

A knock lives 60 seconds at the relay (spec §3.2). The push, where enabled, says “New activity” and nothing else — it cannot say more, because nothing more exists server-side. Edge frame (dashed): knock already lapsed when the app opens.

PurposeAnswerable from anywhere in the app, without yanking the user out of what they were doing. Label + “is knocking.” + two verbs — no preview, because no content exists until the line is bridged.
Entry pointsPeer knocks while this app is open and connected (relay forwards the knock — spec §3.2.2, peer-online branch).
Exit pointsPick up → ACCEPT sent → F5.4 live session for that channel. Ignore → banner leaves; nothing is recorded, the peer only ever observes silence.
Edge casesBanner untouched for 60 s → dismisses itself; no “missed knock” list exists anywhere (that would be stored state). Two channels knock at once → banners queue one at a time, oldest first (product decision, noted in README).

PurposeShow exactly how little a nimna push says, on the surface where other apps leak the most. “New activity” is the complete message — a contentless nudge.
Entry pointsPeer knocked while this phone's app was disconnected AND this channel had opted into notifications (spec §3.2.2 peer-offline branch). Default-off means most users never see this frame.
Exit pointsTap → app opens → app-lock gate first if set (F4.2) → F6.3 if the knock still lives, F6.4 if it lapsed. Ignore → nothing anywhere changes.
Edge casesMultiple knocks / channels → still just one “New activity”; pushes never enumerate. Push arrives long after the knock died (OS delay) → F6.4's honest handling. Push token is the one piece of state that may persist server-side; the spec keeps push optional to protect the zero-persistence claim — mirrored in F7.1's caption.

PurposeA full-screen “incoming call” for the case where the app opens INTO a waiting knock — nothing to catch up on, just answer or don't. Never any message content: none exists anywhere until bridged.
Entry pointsApp opened (from the push, or by habit) while a live knock is registered for one of this phone's channels; shown after the app-lock gate (F4.2) if lock is on.

Edge casesKnock expires while this screen is up (user stared past 0:00) → screen swaps in place to F6.4's copy. Attribution note: the label is known here because the knock is live on the connection — contrast with F6.4.

Knock back
Not now
PurposeArriving seconds too late shouldn't feel like failure. One line of fact, one warm suggestion — knocking back just swaps the roles.
Entry pointsA knock was seen (banner or knock screen) but expired before the user acted — e.g. phone picked up at 0:02, unlocked at −0:05.
Exit pointsKnock back → F5.2 (your own knock on that channel, fresh 60 s). Not now → F4.1.
Edge casesHonesty note: the named variant shown here requires the app to have SEEN the knock while it lived. From a cold open after expiry, the relay (stateless) has nothing to report and the push named no channel — so no screen appears at all, honestly: the app cannot know who knocked. This asymmetry is annotated in the README, not papered over. Knock back to a peer who already left → F5.2 runs its minute and lapses to F5.7; no loop.
